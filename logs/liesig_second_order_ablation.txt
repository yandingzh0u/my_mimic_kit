LieSig-STADD level-2 ablation: what actually removes the Roll shortcut
Date: 2026-08-14

Retraction
----------
The Levy-area hypothesis is falsified. The claim that Roll needs the
antisymmetric level-2 block because it encodes traversal order does not
survive its own control experiment and has been removed from the code.

Setup
-----
Three runs, identical in everything except the trajectory block of the ADD
differential: humanoid_roll cycle env (2 s episodes), 4096 envs, seed 0,
1000 iterations, 131,072,000 samples, stock ADD training (one discriminator,
one zero-vs-policy BCE, one softplus reward, no branches, no fusion, no
auxiliary losses).

  L1       Delta = [state, m_ref - m_sim]                      206 dim
  L2/area  Delta = [state, dm, vech_upper(A_ref - A_sim)]      767 dim
  sym      Delta = [state, dm, vech_upper(S_ref - S_sim)]      767 dim
           with S = 1/2 m m^T on the same strict upper triangle as A

L2 and sym have identical differential width and identical discriminator
parameter count (1,312,513), so they differ in nothing but whether the
level-2 block is antisymmetric or symmetric. S is order-blind: reversing the
traversal order flips A's sign and leaves S untouched (unit-tested).

Behavior, unified protocol (10 s = 300 control steps, 256 episodes, seed 7)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
                    winding mean   median    shortcut       height RMSE
                                                            (success only)
  STADD-v1 (frozen)    +0.924      +0.980    19/256  7.4%      0.0545
  LieSig L1            +0.035      -0.005   256/256  100%      n/a
  LieSig L2 (area)     +0.889      +0.974    27/256 10.5%      0.0582
  LieSig sym           +0.930      +0.976    15/256  5.9%      0.0605

All 256 episodes of all runs completed the full 300 steps; no failures.

The order-blind symmetric block rolls at least as well as the area. Path-order
information is therefore not necessary for Roll. The gap that matters is L1
vs both level-2 arms (100% shortcut vs ~6-10%), which is far outside anything
a seed swap could produce; the differences among v1 / L2 / sym are not, so no
claim is made that sym beats either of them on one seed.

Why the level-2 lift works
--------------------------
Both sides are lifted independently and the ADD differential is taken after
the lift. For the symmetric block that differential is exactly

    Delta_S = 1/4 (dm sm^T + sm dm^T),
    dm = m_ref - m_sim,   sm = m_ref + m_sim

(verified to 5e-7 in float32, unit-tested). A level-1 discriminator only ever
sees dm, so the same tracking error looks identical whether it happens during
a vigorous roll or while lying almost still. After the per-side quadratic lift
it does not: Delta_S is not a function of dm, so this is information regained,
not level 1 re-encoded. Plain ADD subtracts first and discards the absolute
motion context; lifting per side and subtracting afterwards brings it back.

L1 is not numerically dead: its normalized level-1 RMS is 1.05 and its
discriminator geometry is smooth. It simply learns a good non-rolling
shortcut -- position and height track well, root angular velocity error stays
at 1.52, and the winding stays at zero.

Discriminator geometry probe (64 envs, 120 steps, seed 0, alpha = 1)
--------------------------------------------------------------------
  gradient energy share      state    level1   level2
  L1                         0.672    0.328      -
  L2/area                    0.309    0.041    0.650
  sym                        0.252    0.036    0.712

Both level-2 arms allocate the same share to the level-2 block, and the
logit/reward curves along alpha in [0, 1] are smooth in all three runs (no
repeat of the shared-head v2 saturated shell). Note the level-2 share is close
to its dimension share (561/767 = 0.73), so this measures that the block is
used, not that any particular coordinate is preferred.

Conclusion
----------
Final method: state differential + discounted m + symmetric quadratic lifted
differential, trained as stock ADD. "sym" is the default in the code.
The area recursion is kept behind liesig_second_order: "area" so this ablation
stays reproducible; it is not needed in production. L1 is kept as the failed
ablation. Not claimed: that sym is statistically better than STADD-v1 or than
the area on a single seed.
